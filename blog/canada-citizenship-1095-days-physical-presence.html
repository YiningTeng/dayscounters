Citizenship's 1,095 Days Are Not a Bigger 730

October 30, 2026 · 3 min read · CountToday / ResiDays Insights

The short version

  • Citizenship needs 1,095 days physically in Canada in the 5 years before you sign.
  • Days in Canada before PR count at half value, capped at 365 days of credit.
  • Days abroad with a Canadian spouse can save your PR. For citizenship, they count as zero.

Many PR holders treat citizenship as the same count with a bigger number: hold 730 for the card, then keep going to 1,095. But the count is different — different value for your early days, and one exception that disappears. Get a piece wrong and you apply months too early.

How the 1,095 count works

Start from the day you sign the application and look back 5 years. Each day physically in Canada as a PR adds one. Any part of a day counts: leave in the morning, back at night, and the day still counts. Only full days outside come off the total.

Days in Canada before PR — as a student, worker, or visitor — count at half value: two days here buy one day of credit, and the credit is capped at 365 days. At least 730 of the 1,095 must be as a PR, so early credit can never do the whole job.

Two conditions ride along: you must still be a PR who meets the 730-day rule when you apply, and you must have filed taxes for 3 of the 5 years, if you were required to file.

A worked example

Say you arrive as a student on September 1, 2022, spend 800 of the days before PR in Canada, and become a PR on March 1, 2025. Your early days are worth 800 × 0.5 = 400, cut to the 365-day cap. You still need 730 days as a PR. Take 12 full days of US trips after PR, and you cross 1,095 on March 12, 2027 — a date nobody guesses from a calendar.

The spouse exception does not carry over

For PR, some days abroad still count — living outside Canada with a Canadian citizen spouse is the common one. Citizenship has no version of this (Crown servants aside). A year abroad with your citizen spouse adds a year to your PR count and zero here. Not in Canada, no credit.

Case: the student who was closer than he thought

A poster arrived as a student in May 2022, became a PR in February 2025, and had about 10 days of US trips, some same-day. He asked whether his student years counted at all. They do — at half value, under the cap — and the same-day trips never came off his total, because a part-day in Canada counts in full.

Case: visitor days that count, if you can prove them

Another poster's spouse lived in Canada on visitor status while sponsorship was processed. Those days count at half value too. The hard part is proof: visitors often have no exit stamp, so the dates have to be rebuilt from CBSA records, tickets, and bookings before they count on the form.

Quick questions

Do same-day trips to the US hurt my citizenship count?

No. Any part of a day in Canada makes the day count. Only full days outside Canada are left out.

Should I apply the day I reach 1,095?

You can, but IRCC suggests applying with a margin, in case a date is read differently. A few spare weeks cost little.

I met the 730 for PR. Is citizenship just a matter of time?

Not quite. You need 1,095 days in Canada in the 5 years before you sign, at least 730 as a PR, plus tax filings for 3 of the 5 years if you had to file.
